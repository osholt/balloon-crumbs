Balloon Crumbs

Pilot flight planner

This predicts drift; it does not plan a controllable route. Wind and OpenAIP are advisory model/community data. Use an official weather, AIP and NOTAM briefing and the pilot’s own judgement before and during flight.

    Forecast setup

    Choose the day and ground elevation; the optimiser searches the start time.

    The destination search tries the remaining start times on the selected day, a 10–180 minute duration and four altitude controls between launch elevation and 2,000 m MSL. Start time and peak altitude are outputs, not inputs.

    3

    Wind layer

    UKMO UKV forecast via Open-Meteo across a wide 5×5 grid.

    Set a launch point to load wind.

    Arrows are spatially interpolated between the 25 source points across the visible sampling area. They point downwind; labels show km/h. Height levels are metres above mean sea level.

    4

    Destination and landing envelope

    Optimise start time, duration and a changing altitude profile toward a destination.

    Waiting for a wind forecast.

    Set a launch point to calculate the landing envelope.

    The purple boundary is a convex envelope of coarse forecast endpoints, not a guarantee that every point inside it is reachable or suitable for landing. A result within 100 m is marked as a forecast match; its reported time window only applies to that forecast profile. The optimiser does not assess airspace, terrain, legal limits, fuel or the balloon’s actual climb/descent performance.

    5

    Aeronautical chart

    OpenAIP advisory airspace context.

    Chart key and limitations
    Purple/blue areas commonly mark controlled airspace.
    Red areas commonly mark restricted, prohibited or danger areas.
    Orange/yellow areas can mark special-use or caution areas.
    Labels and boundary styles give class and vertical limits.

    Colours are a reading aid, not a legal interpretation. OpenAIP may omit temporary restrictions and current NOTAMs.
