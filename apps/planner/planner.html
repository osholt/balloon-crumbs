    Planner help, safety and data

    The search varies the remaining start times on the selected day, a 10–180 minute duration and four altitude controls up to the selected maximum altitude, while respecting the selected maximum ascent and descent rates. The purple boundary is a convex envelope of coarse forecast endpoints, not a guarantee that every point inside it is reachable or suitable for landing. A result within 100 m is marked as a forecast match. The rate limits are pilot inputs: the planner cannot verify that they suit the balloon, conditions or phase of flight, and it does not assess airspace, terrain, legal limits or fuel.

    Aeronautical chart OpenAIP advisory airspace
    Wind details and chart key

    Wind arrows are spatially interpolated between 25 forecast points. They point downwind and show km/h; heights are metres above mean sea level.

    Purple/blue areas commonly mark controlled airspace.
    Red areas commonly mark restricted, prohibited or danger areas.
    Orange/yellow areas can mark special-use or caution areas.
    Labels and boundary styles give class and vertical limits.

    Colours are a reading aid, not a legal interpretation. OpenAIP may omit temporary restrictions and current NOTAMs.
