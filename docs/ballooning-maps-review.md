# Ballooning Maps review

Reviewed 22 August 2026: [Bath RVP weather outlook](https://weather.ballooningmaps.co.uk/BathRVP.html).

## What is useful

The page is built for quick pre-flight scanning rather than general weather
browsing. Its strongest pattern is the matrix: local forecast time across the
page and altitude down the page. Surface, 150 ft, 500 ft, 1,000 ft and 2,000 ft
wind can be compared without opening separate charts. Surface gust, cloud cover
and base, visibility, rain, temperature, humidity and QNH sit in the same local
flight context.

The forecast run and step are visible. Sunrise and sunset are shown beside the
site-specific outlook, and the page links directly to neighbouring drift maps.
That makes freshness and temporal context part of the primary reading task,
instead of metadata hidden behind an information button.

## What Balloon Crumbs should learn

- Keep a saved catalogue of launch, rendezvous and previous landing areas.
- Offer a compact time-by-altitude wind table before the map detail.
- Put forecast run, valid time, source, units and model step beside every value.
- Include surface gust, visibility, cloud base and QNH in the planning scan.
- Show sunrise/sunset in the launch area's local time zone.
- Let a time slot open the drift envelope and altitude profile without losing
  the table context.
- Keep observations, model forecasts and pilot-entered values visually distinct.

## Boundaries

The page is an inspiration source, not an authority or data licence. Balloon
Crumbs must not scrape or redistribute its presentation or provider data. Any
weather integration still requires a selected licensed source and must expose
timestamp, model/run, units and limitations. Restricted airspace and NOTAM
briefing remain separate official-source tasks.
